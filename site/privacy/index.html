1. Scope and operator

This draft covers the StreamBridge website and application described in the public repository. The project is maintained at sascio/MyBridge; the legal operator, address and private privacy contact are not confirmed. This document is not a representation that legal review or compliance assessment is complete. Third-party services operate under their own policies.

2. Information and purposes

Information depends on features and configuration: local profiles, settings, library and watch progress; account credentials and sync data; service tokens, identifiers, queries, lists and playback activity; and technical and diagnostic data. These are used to run selected functions, authenticate, synchronize, fetch metadata, play media and troubleshoot. The project has not verified the complete scope of data accessible to all operators or any service-side retention.

3. Website, hosting and submissions

The site is a static Cloudflare Pages site. Requests to hosting and security infrastructure can expose IP address, user agent, URL, time and other request metadata to those providers; their logging, cookies and retention depend on deployed configuration and provider practices, which have not been independently verified. No contact form or analytics script was found in the inspected website source; this is not a guarantee about infrastructure-level analytics or other deployments. Public GitHub issues or pull requests may expose what you submit. Do not submit passwords, tokens or sensitive information publicly.

4. Browser storage and third-party requests

The site saves the theme preference in browser localStorage. Clearing site data resets it. The home and News pages fetch GitHub’s API for releases; the home page also requests contributor data and may load GitHub avatar images. Those providers receive ordinary request metadata, including IP address. The supplied video and site assets are served from the site; no external font or iframe was found in the inspected pages. Linked services may use their own cookies and tracking.

5. Account backend and synchronization

Sign-in, profiles and sync can use the default api.nuvio.tv backend, operated by NuvioMedia rather than StreamBridge, or a user-configured backend. Account credentials, account identifiers, profiles, library, watch progress and settings may be sent to the chosen backend when the relevant features are used. Configuration transfer can include selected API keys when credential inclusion is enabled. Its operator’s policy, security and deletion processes apply; the project cannot verify that operator’s retention or access practices.

6. Local storage and credentials

The app stores profiles, settings, library, progress, sources, downloads and some credentials locally. Storage differs by provider and platform: Trakt auth payloads use app-private Android preferences and iOS user defaults; Simkl secrets use Android Keystore-backed storage or iOS Keychain; AniList, MDBList and WeTrakr auth use protected platform persistence. Other keys and caches may use different storage. This is not a blanket encryption or security guarantee. Device backups, exported configuration and downloaded files may persist separately.

7. Trakt

Trakt uses OAuth authorization through a browser or device-code flow. Depending on enabled features the app may read account, library, lists, history, ratings and progress, and send watch activity or changes to Trakt. Authorization data is stored in the app’s profile-scoped local storage. Disconnect in the app and review or revoke access in your Trakt account; local caches and remote records may remain. Trakt processes its own data under its privacy policy.

8. Simkl

Simkl supports OAuth with PKCE or device/PIN authorization. The app may retrieve lists, history, progress and ratings and send selected watch activity or updates. Access and refresh tokens are held in platform-protected local storage, with separate local metadata and caches. Disconnect in the app and review authorization at Simkl; revocation and deletion of remotely held history are controlled by Simkl. The Simkl privacy policy applies.

9. AniList

AniList uses an OAuth implicit-token authorization flow. Depending on your actions, the app can access the account and anime lists, statuses, ratings and episode progress, and write changes. The token is stored in protected local persistence. Disconnect in the app and review access in AniList; disconnecting does not erase the provider’s records or necessarily every local cache. AniList’s policy applies.

10. MDBList

MDBList supports device OAuth requesting write access for account functions, and can also use an API key for ratings. Depending on configuration it may receive media identifiers and queries and exchange account lists, history or changes. OAuth credentials are kept in protected local persistence; an API key may be kept in settings and may be included in credential-bearing configuration transfer. Disconnect clears local authorization and attempts remote revocation, which can fail; remove any separately configured API key and check MDBList’s own controls. The MDBList privacy policy applies.

11. WeTrakr

WeTrakr uses a device-code OAuth flow. Depending on enabled features the app can exchange account details, lists, ratings, history and progress, including changes and watch activity. Access and refresh tokens are stored in protected local persistence. Disconnect clears local authorization and attempts logout; network failure can prevent remote logout. Check the provider’s controls and privacy policy for revocation and deletion.

12. Other network services and diagnostics

Configured addons, plugins, media servers, TMDB, OMDb, IntroDB, subtitle, debrid and live-TV services can receive requests, identifiers, credentials and IP addresses appropriate to the feature. External players may receive selected playback URLs and headers. Optional peer-to-peer playback can expose your IP address to peers and upload data when enabled. Update checks contact GitHub. Android contains Sentry crash reporting: the inspected setting defaults to enabled when supported and a configured DSN is present; it can be turned off in Settings → Advanced. Reports may contain app/device details, errors and network breadcrumbs. The README describes crash reporting as opt-in, which conflicts with the inspected default; release configuration and consent need confirmation. iOS code marks this crash-reporting setting unsupported.

13. Sharing, storage and retention

Data may be sent to services you choose, the configured account backend, hosting, GitHub and configured diagnostics infrastructure. Whether StreamBridge maintainers receive any of that data depends on control of the deployed service or diagnostic account and is not established by source inspection. Provider locations and cross-border processing may vary. No verified retention period is available for those systems, logs, backups or support requests; consult the relevant operator. This draft makes no sale, transfer, deletion or retention guarantee.

14. Disconnecting and deletion

Use integration settings to disconnect, remove configured keys and sources, and review each provider’s account controls. Local authorization removal does not necessarily remove caches, downloaded files, backups, remote records or already-submitted diagnostics. Device storage controls and uninstalling may remove app data, subject to operating-system and backup behavior. Account and synced data deletion must be requested from the operator of the configured backend; deletion outcomes and residual copies are not verified here.

15. Security and children

The code uses platform storage and certain protected credential stores, but no system is completely secure and the project has not verified a security certification. Protect your device and connected accounts. The legal operator’s children’s-data practices and age-verification process are not yet confirmed. Parents and guardians should supervise use where applicable; any legal age and consent requirements remain applicable.

16. Rights and regional requirements

Depending on where you live and the responsible operator, you may have rights to access, correction, erasure, withdrawal of consent, grievance redress or other remedies, subject to applicable law and exceptions. India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and applicable rules may be relevant depending on the processing and provisions in force; applicability, commencement, notices, consent, grievance arrangements and response procedures require qualified review. Other regional laws may also apply. Requests concerning a third party’s records should be directed to that provider.

17. Contact, updates and review

The privacy contact, grievance channel, legal identity and any applicable officer details have not been confirmed. For non-sensitive project questions you can use GitHub issues; issues are public, so do not post personal data, credentials or confidential requests. A private request channel is needed before final adoption. This draft may be updated after factual and legal review; the date records this draft revision, not legal approval.
